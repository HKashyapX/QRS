# QRS v0.2.1 Acquisition Refinement

## Evidence

The first v0.2.0 dual-lane phone run transferred a 223,371-byte file at only 234 bytes/s. It recorded
43.4% marker lock, 13.8% accepted phase pairs, and 4.4 average camera pixels per cell. The camera
delivered 720×1280 at 30 FPS, but the receiver processed a square 720×720 centre crop. Processing
used about 534 ms per second, so compute was not saturated; optical sampling geometry was the first
constraint.

## Changes

- The preview canvas follows the camera stream's aspect ratio. A 720×1280 portrait stream is now
  processed as 720×1280 rather than losing its top and bottom 280 rows.
- Manual fallback dimensions are calculated from the active canvas and lane count.
- Envelope detection uses a 240-pixel downsample target and accepts larger near-full-frame regions.
- After a lane classification failure, the receiver makes at most two additional attempts using
  0.985× and 1.015× quad scales. Successful first attempts pay no retry cost.
- Diagnostics report alignment retries/recoveries and classify sub-5-pixel sampling as the dominant
  bottleneck before transport recovery.
- Requested and granted camera FPS are stored separately. A 60 FPS request that produces a 30 FPS
  track is displayed as a fallback, not an ambiguous unknown.

## Expected result

This change is intended to restore at least six camera pixels per cell when the portrait envelope
fills most of a 720-pixel-wide phone stream. It should raise marker recognition and phase-pair
acceptance without changing optical framing or fountain coding. The actual gain must be established
with the same sender, receiver, file, distance, brightness, and 50 ms phase duration used for the
v0.2.0 diagnostic.

## Next field-test gate

1. Transfer a small file in dual-lane mode at 50 ms/phase and 30 FPS.
2. Confirm `tracked camera pixels/cell avg` is at least 5, preferably 6 or more.
3. Confirm marker lock exceeds 65% and accepted phase pairs exceed 55%.
4. Record alignment retries/recoveries; high retry volume with low recovery points to blur or phase
   timing rather than boundary placement.
5. Only after this baseline improves, repeat with the same 223,371-byte file.

The 150 kbps research target remains far beyond this milestone. v0.2.1 removes a measured regression;
it does not claim that final target.
