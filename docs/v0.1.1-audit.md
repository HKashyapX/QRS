# QRS v0.1.1 Next-Day Audit

## Why this revision exists

The first two-device run proved end-to-end transfer but exposed three dominant acquisition limits:

- orientation markers were the most frequent error;
- low differential cell contrast was second;
- fullscreen erased the detectable boundary because white symbol margin met a white viewport.

At 300 ms per phase, one logical frame also needs at least 600 ms. That is a theoretical maximum of
1.67 logical frames/s before camera loss or decoding. The former 75 ms receiver throttle imposed a
separate ceiling and sampled browser repaint time instead of actual camera frames.

## Implemented changes

| Area | v0.1.1 behavior | Intended effect |
|---|---|---|
| Envelope | Three-cell black guard outside the three-cell white quiet zone | Keeps the white boundary detectable in fullscreen |
| Orientation | Four static 9×9 ring anchors with unique 3×3 IDs | Makes orientation independent of A/B inversion and tolerates local damage |
| Phase | Separate 32-bit pilot split across top and bottom borders | Labels A/B without treating the whole marker field as inverted |
| Capture | `requestVideoFrameCallback` with animation-frame fallback | Processes new camera frames rather than display repaint guesses |
| Tracking | Full detection at most every 100 ms; tracked quad reused between runs | Reduces detector cost while sampling every camera frame |
| Sender timing | Animation-frame deadline scheduler; 33–300 ms choices | Reduces timer drift and exposes a measured timing ladder |
| Diagnostics | Camera FPS, processing latency, detector reuse and ranked errors | Locates the actual bottleneck on each device pair |

The 64×64 payload capacity changes from 462 to 448 bytes because the larger anchors reserve more
cells. A 256-byte fountain symbol plus the current frame envelope still fits.

## Audit checklist

1. Confirm the landing page says `QRS v0.1.1 · Optical Envelope`.
2. In sender fullscreen, confirm a black band surrounds the complete white square on all four sides.
3. Run `payload.txt` at 300 ms and save the expanded receiver diagnostics.
4. Repeat at 220, 150, and 100 ms without changing distance or lighting.
5. For each run, record camera FPS, average/max processing time, valid/rejected pairs, detector
   runs/reused tracks, and the top-errors line.
6. Continue only with the fastest setting where valid frames rise steadily and marker/phase errors
   do not dominate.
7. Test 1 KB, then 10 KB, and compare downloaded bytes with the originals.
8. Repeat the small file in fullscreen and at a moderate oblique angle.

## Interpretation

- Camera FPS below 15 suggests capture/device/browser limits before QRS classification.
- Average processing time close to the camera-frame interval means the main thread is saturated.
- Many detector runs with few reused tracks means geometry is not staying locked.
- Orientation errors with a stable tracked quad point to wrong envelope corners or insufficient cell
  resolution, not the phase scheduler.
- Phase-pilot errors point to transition frames, motion blur, rolling shutter, or display PWM.
- Cell-contrast errors after reliable marker and pilot lock point to focus, glare, exposure, or
  phase timing.
- CRC errors with good marker, pilot, and contrast results point to mismatched phase pairs or
  isolated data-cell errors.

## Known limits

This is still an experimental binary optical link. It has no encryption, authenticated manifest,
adaptive density, colour symbols, autofocus/exposure control, worker-thread pipeline, or
AprilTag-style gradient/line quad detector. The current connected-component detector remains the
most likely replacement target if the guarded envelope does not remain stable across devices.

The original goal of roughly 33 logical frames in two seconds cannot be reached with two phases at
300 ms each. Even at 33 ms per phase the ideal ceiling is about 15 logical frames/s, before losses.
Meeting 33 frames in two seconds therefore requires at least one of: a faster display/camera
channel, more payload per logical frame, or a future single-capture modulation scheme.
